CDL Cohort Rotation Board

Two ways to run the truck fleet against a steady intake of six students every two weeks. Same online funnel, same four trucks and four instructors — the only thing that changes is how long a cohort holds a truck, and that changes everything downstream.

60 hr online · self-led · prerequisite 120 hr behind-the-wheel 4 trucks · 3 students each 10 hr/day Mon–Thu Fri skills testing intake 6 / 2 wks

The two models at a glance

per-student & per-fleet

Because you count all in-truck time toward the 120 hours, a cohort clears its behind-the-wheel requirement in three weeks — Model A. If your ELDT / Wisconsin DOT approval only lets part of the ride-along count (say 30 of the 40 weekly hours), it stretches to four weeks — Model B, the conservative fallback.

Best when you want slack in the system — the idle week per truck absorbs a holiday, a weather day, or maintenance with no ripple, and you have headroom to grow past six.

Model B · 30 of 40 hrs count

4-week truck phase

30 counted hrs/week per student → 120 in 4 weeks

6 wksdoor-to-door / student
100%fleet utilization
6 / 2wkintake (no headroom)
0 wkidle buffer / truck cycle

Best when only partial observation may count. It hits six exactly, but runs flat-out — a lost day pushes that cohort's finish and its test day, since nothing is idle to absorb it.

What holds in both models

The online funnel is identical

Six students begin the 60-hour online course every two weeks — 30 hr/wk, self-led, due the day before truck Day 1. Because it needs no truck, you can start it earlier and stretch it (e.g. 4 wks @ 15 hr/wk) if 30 hr/wk is too heavy for working adults; the only hard rule is done before the wheel.

Friday testing never bottlenecks

A finishing pair is 6 students. The two instructors who taught them are excluded, leaving two eligible examiners — 3 tests each × ~2 hr, staggered across the 10-hour Friday. Trucks don't run Fridays, so every instructor is free to test.

Instructor load differs

Model A oscillates 2↔4 trucks active by week (some instructors get a lighter week). Model B keeps all 4 instructors on a truck every week — full, steady, no cushion. Either way it's one instructor per truck; a second daily shift isn't possible with four.

Holidays & the ramp

Weeks 1–2 only run two trucks while trucks 3–4 wait for their first online cohort. On holidays, Model A's idle week absorbs a closure cleanly; in Model B a closure shifts that cohort's finish and Friday test — plan make-ups around Fall Break (Oct 16–19 on your campus calendar).

On the real calendar

anchored to W1 = Mon Oct 5, 2026

Here's the finding that jumps out once you drop it onto actual dates: because driving is Mon–Thu, every Friday and weekend holiday costs zero driving time. Only breaks that include a weekday Mon–Thu — Thanksgiving Thursday, and full-week closures — actually reduce capacity.

Sep 7
Labor Day (Mon) — before intakeFalls in the online on-ramp window; no truck impact. If a Monday closure did land in a driving week it would cost one 10-hr day.
Oct 16
Fall Break + Employee Prep (Fri) — freeFriday only. No Mon–Thu driving lost. No cohort tests that Friday in either model. Costs nothing.
Nov 26–27
Thanksgiving (Thu + Fri) — costs a dayLoses one 10-hr driving day. Model A absorbs it in a truck's turnaround week. Model B can't — cohort E·F ends 10 hrs short and its Fri Nov 27 test collides with the next cohort's start.
Dec 21–Jan 1
Winter Break (~2 weeks) — planned pauseAny cohort straddling it stretches by two weeks. The clean fix in both models: don't launch a cohort that would span the break — take a deliberate ~3-week intake pause and resume in January.

Model B's test cadence happens to land on Nov 27 and Dec 25 at this start date (the ⚠ flags) — shifting the program's start Monday can dodge that, but the underlying point stands: Model A can move a test with no cascade, Model B can't.

Annual outlook

Model A over a year

The 25% turnaround slack is the hero: single-day holidays vanish into it, and the ~8/2wk ceiling means a post-break backlog can be caught up. One planned ~3-week pause at Winter Break is the only calendar-driven gap. Highest theoretical throughput, most resilient.

Model B over a year

At 100% utilization there's nothing to catch up with, so every closure must become a planned intake gap — Thanksgiving week and Winter Break both. That trims annual output and demands disciplined test-day make-ups. Choose it only if compliance forces the 4-week phase.

Student counts assume the "all in-truck time counts toward the 120" basis you specified — an ELDT / Wisconsin DOT determination. If only partial observation counts, Model B's numbers are the realistic ceiling.
